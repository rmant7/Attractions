Kaunas

Kaunas, the second-largest city in Lithuania, is a hidden gem for budget travellers seeking an authentic cultural experience. This vibrant city boasts a rich history and a unique blend of architectural styles, from Gothic to Art Deco. The city's Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with its charming cobblestone streets, colorful buildings, and picturesque squares.

Kaunas is also known for its lively festivals, such as the Kaunas Jazz Festival and the Pažaislis Music Festival, which showcase the city's love for music and the arts. The city's museums and galleries, including the M. K. Čiurlionis National Museum of Art and the Devil's Museum, offer a glimpse into Lithuania's rich cultural heritage.

Foodies will delight in the local cuisine, which features hearty dishes like cepelinai (potato dumplings stuffed with meat or cheese) and kibinai (savory pastries filled with meat or vegetables). The city's bustling markets, such as the Kaunas Farmers' Market, offer a chance to sample local delicacies and mingle with the friendly locals.

One of the most surprising facts about Kaunas is that it was once the temporary capital of Lithuania during the interwar period, and its architecture reflects this period of prosperity and innovation. Visitors can explore the city's many Art Deco buildings, such as the iconic Christ's Resurrection Church, which boasts a stunning interior and panoramic views of the city.

For budget travellers, Kaunas offers plenty of free or low-cost attractions, such as the Kaunas Castle, the Ninth Fort Museum, and the Žaliakalnis Funicular Railway. The city's parks and green spaces, such as the Kaunas Botanical Garden and the Pazaislis Monastery Park, offer a peaceful escape from the hustle and bustle of the city.

In conclusion, Kaunas is a city that truly has something for everyone. Its rich cultural heritage, vibrant festivals, delicious cuisine, and stunning architecture make it a must-visit destination for budget travellers seeking an authentic and unforgettable experience.
